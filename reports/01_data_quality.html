Severity

Critical
Ignoring it makes the headline answers wrong (e.g. the active base or annual-plan counts).
High
Ignoring it materially distorts at least one leadership question.
Medium
Distorts a specific cut or the churn definition; must be handled in the clean layer.

Type

Data Bug
The data in the file is missing or wrong. A query can flag or estimate it, but cannot fully restore it.
Analytical Gotcha
The data is correct, but it misleads an analyst who does not know its structure. One rule handles it fully.

Handled completely in the clean layer; nothing is lost.
Partial
Estimated or kept with a flag; some uncertainty remains.
None
Cannot be repaired from the data; shown as 'Unknown' or replaced by our own rule.

What is broken. Built from each user's last transaction up to 2017-02-28, the 'due in March' set and the labels disagree: some labelled users fall outside it (L6), some users in it are unlabelled (L7), and 639 labelled users have no transaction up to 2017-02-28 (L4).

#2Critical Data BugFix: Partial T9transactions.payment_plan_days, list_price Q1 Q3 Q4

Plan length and list price missing (0) on paid rows, Mar-Oct 2015

214,207rows · 3.74%
122,393users · 20.2%
  • T9 paid rows missing plan length and list price 214,207

What is broken. payment_plan_days = 0 and list_price = 0, but amount_paid > 0 (194,047 rows paid 149) and expires_at is about 1 month later. All rows fall in 2015-03 to 2015-10: a logging gap in the billing system, not free plans. Date evidence: 86.4% end 25-35 days after the purchase (normal paid 30-day rows: 90.1%); the 1,031 rows paid 1,788 run a median 413 days, i.e. annual plans.

Why it matters (Q1, Q3, Q4). In Mar-Oct 2015 these rows belong to 39.7% of active users (121,000 of 305,113). Treated as 0-day plans they create no subscription period, so that share of the base vanishes from the 2015 trend (Q1). Left as is, 2015 also shows 0-day plans, a negative discount (paid > list) and broken ARPU per plan. That corrupts the 2015 baseline for the trend (Q1), price points (Q3) and plan mix (Q4).

#3Critical Analytical GotchaFix: Full T2, T1, T3transactions.expires_at, src_file Q1 Q4

The two exports are split on expiry date, not on transaction date

89,728rows · 1.57%
65,306users · 10.78%
  • T2 old purchases in export_2 89,728
  • T1 export_1 rows expiring after March 2017 0
  • T3 long plans only in export_2 15,731

What is broken. export_1 contains only rows with expires_at <= 2017-03-31: T1 finds 0 rows expiring later. export_2 contains every row expiring after that: 266,538 March 2017 purchases plus 89,728 purchases made 2015 - Feb 2017. Neither file is complete on its own.

Why it matters (Q1, Q4). Reading export_2 as 'the March file' drops every subscription bought earlier that is still alive after March 2017. That undercounts the active base at period end (Q1) and hides almost all recent annual plans: 10,228 of the 10,291 410-day plans bought Aug 2016 - Feb 2017 sit in export_2 (Q4).

SELECT src_file, max(expires_at), count(*) FILTER (WHERE expires_at > 20170331) FROM raw_transactions GROUP BY 1;

Incremental append: export_1 + March rows of export_2

Rejected

Standard monthly-load pattern: export_1 is history, export_2 is the new month; append export_2 rows with transacted_at >= 2017-03-01.

Run cost
Lowest (filtered append)

Lowest

#4High Data BugFix: Partial T23, L5transactions.user_id Q1 Q2

Transacting users missing from users.csv

697,306rows · 12.18%
109,330users · 18.05%
  • T23 users missing from users.csv 697,306
  • L5 labelled user missing from users.csv 27,606

What is broken. 109,330 users with transactions (18.05% of transacting users; 697,306 rows) have no row in the user registry. They carry 71.7M of revenue (8.3% of 862.6M); 53,023 of them paid at least once. 27,606 labelled users are also missing, and they are not a random subset: they churn at 5.2% vs 9.4% for the 214,059 labelled users found in the registry.

Why it matters (Q1, Q2). An inner join to users silently drops 18% of transacting users and 8.3% of revenue from every metric (Q1). Because the missing users churn at about half the rate of the rest, an inner join also biases the churn rate and every churn-driver cut (Q2). Their attributes (age, channel, city) are unknown, so segment cuts cover only part of the base. It ranks below the Critical issues because it does not lose rows by itself (revenue and actives come from transactions), but any join to users silently changes both the totals and the churn answer.

#5High Data BugFix: None U11, U1, U2, U3, U4users.age Q2

Age missing or implausible

1,136,998rows · 67.22%
1,136,998users · 67.22%
  • U11 age unusable: outside 4-92 1,136,998
  • U1 age = 0, the missing-value code 1,135,039
  • U2 negative age 72
  • U3 age 1-3 37
  • U4 age above 92 1,850

What is broken. age = 0 for 67.1% of users (the missing-value code), plus negative ages (min -6,807), very young and very old ages (max 1,820). Range kept: 4-92, deliberately wide so real users are not dropped (the app is for all ages). Lower bound: ages 1-3 (37 users) look partly like unfilled profiles: 21.6% have an empty profile (gender missing and city = 1), against 94.2% at age 0 and 0.9% at 13-92; the count also jumps from 9 at age 3 to 28 at 4. Upper bound: ages 81-92 stay at 28-51 users per year, like the late 70s; from 93 the counts rise again (106: 189, 112: 324), which is not demographic. There is no ground truth, so we cannot tell whether either edge should be earlier or later; the edge groups (4-12: 311; 81-92: 454) are small, and a sensitivity check can drop them.

Why it matters (Q2). Age is one of the churn segments (Q2). Taking 0 or 1,820 at face value breaks the age bands. The users with a known age are a non-random minority, so a naive cut is confounded.
