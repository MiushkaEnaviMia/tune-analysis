▶️ 1. How to run it end to end

All code is in the GitHub repository: numbered SQL scripts in sql/, Python steps in py/, reports in reports/. To rebuild everything from the raw CSVs:

  1. Clone the repository and run pip install duckdb openpyxl (Python 3.9+)
  2. Put users.csv, transactions_export_1.csv, transactions_export_2.csv and churn_labels.csv in data/ at the repository root
  3. Run python run_all.py from the repository root. It runs every sql/NN_*.sql in order into tune.duckdb (~20 s), then every py/NN_*.py, which rewrites the Part 1a reports

The repository holds no data/ folder and no database: all paths are relative, so the scripts run on your own copy of the CSVs

1b · GrainChoose the subscription table's grain and explain why

Grain: Period + episode_id

Chosen: fct_subscription has one row per billing period, and every row carries episode_id

Why Period + episode_id

  • Monthly revenue, ARPU and discounts (Part 2): shows how much money came in each month and how price or discount changed from period to period
  • Plan changes (Upgrade / Downgrade): every billing period can be compared to the previous one, as the brief asks
  • Keeps the Episode advantage: episode_id identifies Reactivated users (Reactivated) and when a new subscription run starts

Trade-off

  • Bigger table: 5,501,434 periods instead of 748,144 episodes
  • The length of a whole subscription run needs GROUP BY user_id, episode_id instead of reading one ready row

Why we still chose it

  • One-way aggregation (lossless vs lossy): from Period + episode_id you can always go up to Episode with a simple GROUP BY; collapsing to Episode first deletes the monthly detail and plan changes, and they cannot be rebuilt from that table
  • Low running cost vs lost flexibility: with episode_id ready on the table and a modern engine (DuckDB), keeping the detail costs little (size, run time); choosing Episode would force rebuilding the model from the raw layer

Options compared

Key fact behind the dates: expires_at is the membership expiry after the event, not the plan’s own end. An early renewal stacks its days on top of the old expiry, so each period also gets non-overlapping covered_start / covered_end (0 overlaps). A new episode starts when the gap after expiry is more than 30 days

+ Known limits

What the rules cannot settle
  • The same-day order (cancel before purchase) is a rule, not a fact: the files have no timestamp or event ID. It is documented, deterministic and has one data-driven exception (a cancel on the first purchase day, issue 11)
  • T16/T17 derived expiries use purchase date + plan days. For an early renewal the true expiry would be later; this affects 1,691 rows
  • March-2017 churn is censored by construction (the 30-day window needs April data). Billing's labels use April data we don't have; Part 1c deals with that
  • Cancel-row amounts (160,720 rows, 23.2M) are read as the cancelled plan's price, not a payment: interpreted, not documented. If billing confirms they are refunds, revenue needs a negative adjustment
  • Left-censoring: data starts 01-01-15, so the 133,585 users first seen in 01-15 may have subscribed earlier. Their "new" is flagged (flag_first_seen_jan2015); New counts in 01-15 must not be read as acquisition

+ Definitions: how each group is counted

Each calendar month (e.g. 03-17) we take every user who had a paid subscription, not a free trial, valid on at least one day of that month (is_active_paid* = 1). Each of them goes into exactly one group, by the purchases they made that month:

  • New (new_paid*): the user's first-ever purchase in the data was made this month (purchase number period_no* = 1)
  • Reactivated (reactivated_paid*): the user made a purchase this month more than 30 days after the previous subscription ended (gap_days* > 30, a new episode_id*). If the user is also New this month, they count as New
  • Renewing (renewing_paid*): every other paying user. They bought again within 30 days of the previous end (early renewal included), or are still inside a plan bought before this month

The three always add up: new_paid + reactivated_paid + renewing_paid = paying_subscribers (checked for all 27 months). Share = group ÷ paying_subscribers, Count = number of users in the group

The data has no 2014 history, so in 01-15 everyone who paid counts as New, and 02-15 and 03-15 still show extra New. 01-15 is left out of the averages

5a · SQLOptional: the SQL to analyze the test

What SQL would analyze it?

Joins experiment_assignments to our clean layer; tested on a fake assignment

Show the SQL 
with a as (
  select e.user_id, e.variant, e.assigned_at::date as d0, max(s.is_auto_renew) as is_auto_renew
  from experiment_assignments e
  left join fct_subscription s
    on s.user_id = e.user_id and e.assigned_at::date between s.start_date and s.end_date
  group by all
), u as (
  select a.user_id, a.variant, a.is_auto_renew,
    max((s.plan_days >= 360 and s.start_date between a.d0 and a.d0 + 37)::int)            as took_annual,
    max((s.is_free = 0 and s.start_date between a.d0 and a.d0 + 37)::int)                 as renewed,
    coalesce(sum(s.amount_paid) filter (where s.start_date between a.d0 and a.d0 + 364), 0) as cash_12m,
    coalesce(sum(s.amount_paid * greatest(0, least(s.end_date, a.d0 + 90) - greatest(s.start_date, a.d0))
                 / nullif(s.end_date - s.start_date, 0)), 0)                               as earned_90d
  from a left join fct_subscription s on s.user_id = a.user_id and s.start_date >= a.d0
  group by all
), g as (
  select is_auto_renew, variant, count(*) n, avg(took_annual) take_up, avg(renewed) renewal,
         avg(cash_12m) cash, var_samp(cash_12m) v, avg(earned_90d) earned_90d
  from u group by all
)
select t.is_auto_renew, t.n n_treat, c.n n_ctrl, t.take_up, c.renewal ctrl_renewal, t.renewal treat_renewal,
       t.cash - c.cash as cash_diff,
       (t.cash - c.cash) / sqrt(t.v / t.n + c.v / c.n) as z_score,   -- |z| > 1.96 = significant
       t.earned_90d - c.earned_90d as earned_90d_diff
from g t join g c on c.is_auto_renew = t.is_auto_renew and t.variant = 'treatment' and c.variant = 'control';

How healthy is the base, what drives churn, did trials and prices work, and should we push annual plans?

Answer: The base is healthy but growth is slowing, and the biggest lever is keeping subscribers, not finding new ones

Churn comes from how people pay and from the first renewal, so we recommend auto-renew by default, a stronger first month and daily checks for failed payments (4.2) · free trials show no clear lift in New subscribers (not fully measured) · annual plans look better but need the 5a test before we push them

4.2 · Three Recommendations

Each with the impact we expect, how sure we are, and what would change our mind

Which three moves do we recommend, with impact, confidence and what would change our mind?

Answer: Three moves: auto-renew by default, win the first renewal and catch payment failures early, together keeping up to ~16,000 subscribers a year

~16,000 = about 5% of today's 303,000 paying subscribers, if each move works as estimated

The Three Moves

Payment failures are the biggest single win, and all three moves target groups that churn at least twice as much as the rest (4.1)

MoveExpected impactConfidenceWhat would change our mind
1 · Move people who pay by hand to auto-renew
Auto-renew on by default at checkout, and offered at every renewal paid by hand. People who pay by hand are 13% of renewals but 52% of churn		~3,000 kept a year for each 10% of them moved, if they churn half as much as todayMedium: auto-renew users may be more committed to begin withA default-on test with no drop in churn, or more refunds and complaints
2 · Win the first renewal
Onboarding and reminders in the first month: 17.1% leave at the first renewal vs ~4.5% later (9.6% even with auto-renew)		~5,000 kept a year (~400 a month) if first-renewal churn falls by a quarterMedium: the gap holds with the same payment mix (2.8×); no usage data to say whyFirst-month leavers turn out to be discount or trial hunters who never meant to stay
3 · Catch payment failures before they become churn
Check failed auto-renew payments by provider every day, retry failed charges, and ask lapsed users to update their payment method		~7,900 kept a year by closing the method 40 vs 41 gap, and no repeat of 03-16 (16,468 users, 7.0% of paying subscribers)Medium-high for incidents (one provider, 97% came back); medium for method 40Billing logs showing deliberate cancellations or a planned provider migration

Not worth acting on: targeting by age (1.3×), city or channel (1.7–1.8×), or discouraging long plans. Before any churn review, use our validated rule (3 days Auto-renew, 30 days Pays by hand), not churn_labels.csv (13.6% of labels wrong). Source: reports/05_deep_dive.md
